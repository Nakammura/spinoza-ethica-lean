## Secondary literature on Spinoza commentary

Bennett, Jonathan. 1984. *A Study of Spinoza's Ethics*.
Indianapolis: Hackett. [verified — full-text PDF audited. The
central loci cited are §17 "No shared attribute" (pp. 65-70) on
Proposition V's *demonstratio*, §18 "Spinoza's monism: the
official argument" (pp. 70-) on Proposition XIV, §16 "Attributes
in Spinoza" (pp. 60-66) on attribute-substance logical form, and
§8 (p. 32) on the rationalist reading of axiom 1a3. Verbatim
quotes used in this paper — "one dubious move and one invalid
one" (p. 67), "flatly invalid move" (p. 68), "cannot yield more
than the conclusion that two substances could not have all their
attributes in common" (p. 69), "wonders how [Spinoza] could
overlook such a gap" (p. 69), "Spinoza's chief use of p5 is in
an argument which has plenty of other things wrong with it"
(p. 70), "a poor thing" (p. 70) — all checked against the 1984
Hackett printing.]

Curley, Edwin, ed. and trans. 1985. *The Collected Works of
Spinoza*. Vol. 1. Princeton: Princeton University Press.
[verified — Curley's editorial discussion of *sive* (e.g.
fn. 36 on Letter 4) treats it as "the 'or' of identity,"
supporting the identifying reading we adopt for *ejusdem
naturae sive attributi* in §3.2. No verbatim text from this
volume is quoted in the present paper.]

Della Rocca, Michael. 2008. *Spinoza*. Routledge Philosophers.
London: Routledge. [verified — full-text PDF audited.
Ch. 2 §3 "The Argument for Substance Monism" (pp. 46–53) is
the central locus for the PSR-based derivation of Proposition V;
Ch. 2 §5 "Necessitarianism" (pp. 69–75) develops the
necessitarian reading. Verbatim quotes used in this paper —
"Non-identities, by the PSR, require explanation, and the way
to explain non-identity is to appeal to some difference in
properties" (p. 47), "thoroughgoing commitment to the PSR"
(p. 1) — checked against the 2008 Routledge printing. The
absence of an explicit modal-logic system (S5, possible-world
machinery) in Della Rocca's prose is also confirmed.]

Della Rocca, Michael. 2002. "Spinoza's Substance Monism." In
*Spinoza: Metaphysical Themes*, edited by Olli Koistinen and
John Biro, 11–37. New York: Oxford University Press. [verified
via Garrett 2018, ch. 3 Postscript, which engages this article
directly under the *Positive* / *Negative* aspect framing
(p. 91). Cited as the canonical statement of the PSR-based
reconstruction whose monograph-form expansion is Della Rocca
2008.]

Garrett, Don. 1990. "Ethics IP5: Shared Attributes and the Basis
of Spinoza's Monism." In *Central Themes in Early Modern
Philosophy: Essays Presented to Jonathan Bennett*, edited by
J. A. Cover and Mark Kulstad, 69–107. Indianapolis: Hackett.
[verified — reprinted as ch. 3 of Garrett 2018 (pp. 62–90), with
publication info confirmed in the 2018 OUP edition. The two-
objection structure (Hooker-Bennett at "2. Differences of
Affections", Leibniz-Bennett at "4. Differences of Attributes")
matches the body of the 1990 essay.]

Garrett, Don. 2018. *Nature and Necessity in Spinoza's
Philosophy*. New York: Oxford University Press. [verified —
full-text PDF audited. Ch. 3 (pp. 62–90) reprints Garrett 1990;
ch. 3 Postscript (pp. 91–97) engages Della Rocca 2002 directly,
distinguishing *Positive* (modes are in and conceived through
their substance) and *Negative* (substance is not in and not
conceived through its modes) aspects of the substance–mode
asymmetry, and arguing that the *Positive*-based replies more
likely capture Spinoza's own thinking. Verbatim quotes used in
this paper — title (cover, title page, LCC), Postscript phrases
*Positive* / *Negative* (p. 91) — checked against the 2018 OUP
printing.]

de Moura, Leonardo, and Sebastian Ullrich. 2021. "The Lean 4
Theorem Prover and Programming Language." In *Automated
Deduction — CADE 28*, edited by André Platzer and Geoff
Sutcliffe, 625–635. Lecture Notes in Computer Science 12699.
Cham: Springer. [verified — DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-79876-5_37;
canonical reference for Lean 4 design.]

Werner, Benjamin. 1997. "Sets in Types, Types in Sets." In
*Theoretical Aspects of Computer Software (TACS '97)*, edited
by Martín Abadi and Takayasu Ito, 530–546. Lecture Notes in
Computer Science 1281. Berlin: Springer. [verified — DOI
10.1007/BFb0014566; cited in §4 for the inaccessible-cardinal
relative consistency result that licenses Lean's kernel as a
foundation no weaker than ZFC.]
